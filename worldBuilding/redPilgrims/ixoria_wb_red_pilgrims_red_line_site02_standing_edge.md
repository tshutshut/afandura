# The Standing Edge

The Second Wound lies upon the face of an immense cliff on one of the islands of the southeastern reaches. The cliff rises almost sheer from the sea, extending for a great distance along the coast. From the water below, its upper edge appears impossibly high. From the land above, the lower reaches are often hidden by mist.

The cliff is not barren. Water runs down its face from the highlands above, gathering in pools and narrow channels before falling in long waterfalls toward the sea. Trees and shrubs grow from ledges and cracks in the stone, while birds nest in the higher recesses and other creatures inhabit the sheltered shelves farther below. Paths, platforms, ropes, and bridges maintained by the Vowed pass through this vertical landscape, connecting the monastery above with the pilgrimage stations below.

Across the middle of the cliff runs a vast band of red stone. It extends along the face of the island for a great distance, following the natural shape of the rock. The band can be seen from far out at sea and is regarded by the Red Pilgrims as the visible mark of the Second Wound.

The monastery of the Vowed stands upon the land above the cliff. This is where pilgrims arrive and prepare for the rite. Unlike at the Open Hollow, they do not surrender their possessions or receive new garments. They continue to wear the red robes received at the previous Wound.

Before beginning the descent, each pilgrim is given a hammock and a length of rope. The hammock is made to serve as a small suspended shelter, with a simple canopy that provides some protection from rain and mist while remaining open to the air. The rope is carried by the pilgrim throughout the descent.

The pilgrim is then secured to the descent system maintained by the Vowed. Through a network of ropes, anchors, and pulleys, they are gradually lowered down the face of the cliff. The descent passes through the living landscape of the Standing Edge, sometimes beneath waterfalls, through mist, and across the narrow paths and platforms that connect the lower stations.

When the red band is reached, the pilgrim is brought to one of the prepared stations. Three large iron hoops are fixed permanently into the rock at each station. The pilgrim attaches the hammock to these points, securing the suspended shelter against the cliff.

There the rite begins.

The pilgrim must remain within the hammock for three days and three nights. Food and water sufficient for the period are provided before the Vowed withdraw. The pilgrim may sleep, eat, drink, pray, or meditate, but must remain at the station throughout the rite.

There is no complete shelter from the elements. Wind passes freely around the hammock. Mist and rain may enter beneath the canopy, and the temperature can fall sharply as clouds gather against the cliff. At other times the sky clears, leaving the pilgrim exposed to the open air with the sea far below.

A permanent rope is installed at every pilgrimage station. It runs upward to the monastery and is separate from the rope carried by the pilgrim. At the monastery, the system terminates in a small bell.

If the pilgrim is in distress, they may pull the permanent rope. The Vowed will then bring them back to the monastery. The rope is always answered.

But calling for the Vowed ends the rite.

A pilgrim who is brought back before the three days and three nights are complete must begin the rite again from the beginning. They cannot return to the Red Line having only partially completed the three days.

For some pilgrims, the Standing Edge is completed on the first attempt. Others return to the monastery after hours or days and descend again when they are ready. It is not uncommon for a pilgrim to make several attempts before completing the rite. The Vowed do not regard this as shameful. The rite is difficult, and the decision to return is left to the pilgrim.

When the three days and three nights have passed, the pilgrim pulls the permanent rope. The small bell at the monastery rings, announcing that the rite has been completed. The Vowed then begin the ascent and bring the pilgrim back to the monastery above.

The Standing Edge is not a place of darkness or enclosure. It is a place of exposure. The pilgrim remains suspended against the red stone while the wind, rain, mist, sunlight, and sea continue around them.

The Second Wound is endured by remaining still.
