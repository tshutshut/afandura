# The Crimson Reach

The Fourth Wound lies near the heart of a fertile island where the land itself appears to change as one travels inland. Near the coast, there is little to distinguish the island from others in the southeastern reaches. Its grasses are green and brown, its trees carry ordinary foliage, and insects and small animals display the colors expected of their kind. The transformation begins gradually. Red appears first in small and easily overlooked places: along the veins of leaves, at the tips of grasses, across patches of lichen, or upon the shells and wings of insects. Farther inland these traces become increasingly pronounced, until scarlet, crimson, burgundy, and deep wine-red begin to dominate the living landscape.

The Vowed call the innermost region the Crimson Reach. Within it, green becomes uncommon and almost every living thing bears some expression of red. Grasses grow crimson from root to tip, leaves deepen into shades of scarlet and burgundy, and mosses and flowering plants spread across the earth in similar hues. Insects and many of the small animals that make their homes there display the same strange coloration. None of this vegetation appears diseased or diminished. On the contrary, the Crimson Reach is remarkably fertile, crowded with plant and animal life that seems perfectly adapted to the transformation.

The settlement maintained by the Vowed stands well outside the Crimson Reach, toward the periphery of the island and close to the routes by which pilgrims normally arrive. Its architecture differs noticeably from that of many other monasteries along the Red Line. Buildings are shaped around curves and rounded spaces, with few straight passages, sharp corners, or perpendicular walls. Red pigments are used throughout the settlement, but never uniformly. Buildings nearer its outer edge bear only faint washes of color, while those facing inland are painted in increasingly rich shades of red, deliberately reproducing in miniature the transformation that occurs across the island.

Much of the work of the Vowed takes place beneath this settlement. Over generations they have excavated an extensive series of underground chambers where specimens from across the island are collected, preserved, compared, and studied. Plants are cultivated in different soils and at different distances from the Crimson Reach. Leaves, roots, seeds, fungi, insects, feathers, water, and earth are carefully examined, while pigments and extracts are prepared from the island's unusual life. Records maintained there extend across generations of Vowed, who regard the patient study of the Fourth Wound as a form of devotion in its own right. Despite this long labor, the cause and nature of the island's transformation remain uncertain.

Pilgrims arriving at the settlement are not subjected to fasting, burdens, silence, or physical trials. When their time comes, they simply leave the settlement and follow the path inland. The journey itself is gentle, passing through thriving countryside as the colors around them slowly change. By the time they enter the Crimson Reach, the transformation is nearly complete, and eventually the immense canopy at the center of the Wound becomes visible above the surrounding vegetation.

At its heart stands the Everred, an ancient tree of extraordinary size. Its immense trunk is covered in deeply furrowed bark of such dark red that the deepest crevices appear almost black. Above, the trunk divides repeatedly into great branches, which divide again and again as they spread outward and upward, producing a broad, almost level canopy reminiscent of an enormous living umbrella. The upper surfaces of its leaves are a lustrous dark crimson, while their undersides are pale silver. When wind moves through the canopy, great sections of the crown seem to change color as thousands of leaves turn together, sending ripples of silver through the red.

The Everred also bears fruit, though neither the Vowed nor generations of pilgrims have discerned a reliable pattern in what it produces. At one time its branches may carry clusters of small dark berries, while at another they may bear heavy pale fruits with thick skins, soft red-fleshed fruits, or varieties unlike those recorded in previous years. More than one kind may sometimes appear upon different branches at once. Whatever their form, the fruits that fall naturally from the Everred may be eaten without any known ill effect. Pilgrims are permitted to gather them from the ground, but they do not pluck them from the branches or damage the tree in order to obtain them.

Seventeen ancient standing stones encircle the Everred at a considerable distance from its trunk. They were not raised by the Red Pilgrims, and their origin is unknown. Some stand straight while others lean, have sunk partially into the earth, or bear cracks accumulated over an unknowable span of time. The Vowed preserve the site but do not restore the stones or attempt to correct their positions. The Everred's enormous canopy now extends almost to the circle they describe, creating a vast shaded space beneath its branches. No one can say with certainty whether the stones were positioned according to the tree, whether the tree grew according to their circle, or whether the correspondence has some other explanation.

The rite of the Fourth Wound is among the simplest along the Red Line. A pilgrim must walk from the settlement to the Everred, enter the circle between any two of the seventeen stones, and eventually return. There is no prescribed route through the circle and no required period of meditation. Once beneath the canopy, pilgrims may rest, sleep, eat, speak with one another, explore, or simply sit beneath the branches for as long as they wish. They are required only to avoid harming or unnecessarily disturbing the life of the Crimson Reach.

There is also no prescribed moment at which they must leave. A pilgrim completes the rite only by deciding, without instruction or assistance, to rise and walk back through the standing stones. Before pilgrims depart the settlement, the Vowed make clear that nobody will be sent to retrieve them. There is no final bell, no deadline, and no party dispatched after a certain number of nights. However long someone remains beneath the Everred, the decision to continue along the Red Line must remain their own. Most eventually make that decision, but some never do.

Those claimed by the Crimson Reach do not appear to suffer. They remain beneath the canopy, sometimes for days or considerably longer, until eventually they lie down to sleep and simply do not wake again. Their bodies are not carried from the site. Moss, lichens, creeping plants, roots, and fallen leaves begin to overtake the remains with unusual speed, until the dead are gradually incorporated into the abundant life surrounding them. There is no petrification or grotesque transformation. With time, little may remain visible beyond a scrap of faded red cloth among the vegetation or an occasional piece of bone emerging between roots.

Pilgrims are expected to leave these remains undisturbed, just as they leave the other life of the Reach undisturbed. Consequently, the true nature of the place often reveals itself only gradually. Someone resting beneath the magnificent red-and-silver canopy might notice an old sleeve beneath a flowering vine, or realize that what appeared to be a pale branch among the moss is bone. Beauty and death coexist quietly beneath the Everred, with neither appearing to diminish the other.

There is, however, one pilgrim who has remained beneath the canopy far longer than any other known to the Vowed. His name is Tomash, though few pilgrims now call him by it. He is an ordinary human who entered the Fourth Wound decades ago and has never completed the return journey. His hair and beard have grown long, his robes have deteriorated repeatedly, and the devout occasionally bring him new clothing. Yet Tomash remains remarkably healthy. He eats, sleeps, walks beneath the tree, converses readily with visitors, and displays none of the physical decline that might be expected from the length of his stay. He appears to have sustained himself largely on the fallen fruit of the Everred throughout his residence beneath its canopy, eating whatever variety the tree happens to produce and seeming to find nothing remarkable in its changing forms.

Tomash remembers his name and much of the life he lived before reaching the Fourth Wound. He remembers his earlier journey along the Red Line and can speak naturally about places and people from his past. What he appears unable to comprehend is the amount of time that has passed since he entered the Crimson Reach. Events separated by years or decades seem close together in his memory, and people who visited long ago may be spoken of as though they departed only recently. He does not behave as though he has chosen to abandon the pilgrimage. When asked, he continues to insist quite sincerely that he intends to leave soon: after another night's sleep, perhaps after breakfast, or when the weather is favorable. Tomorrow, usually.

Generations of pilgrims have therefore come to know him affectionately as Old Tomorrow. The Vowed use another name in the records they have maintained throughout his extraordinary residence beneath the Everred. There, across years of observations and the hands of successive record-keepers, Tomash is known simply as the Long Guest.
