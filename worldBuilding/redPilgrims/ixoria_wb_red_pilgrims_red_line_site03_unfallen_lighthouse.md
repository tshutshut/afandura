# The Unfallen Lighthouse

The Third Wound lies among the ruins of an ancient lighthouse on one of the islands of the southeastern reaches. Little remains of the original structure at ground level. Its lower walls collapsed long ago, leaving broken masonry, shattered stairs, and broad fields of rubble scattered around the site. Yet the upper portion of the lighthouse never fell. Several levels of the old tower, including the remains of its lantern chamber, hang suspended high above the ruins. No visible structure supports them, and the broken section remains fixed in place above the Veilstep that stands among the remains below, unchanged by wind, rain, or the passage of years.

No known path reaches the suspended lighthouse. Its interior has not been entered within living memory, and the Red Pilgrims make no attempt to reach it. To them, the Third Wound lies not above, but among the ruins beneath. Around these ruins, the Vowed have established a small monastic settlement. Their dwellings, kitchens, storehouses, courtyards, and places of prayer have grown slowly between the fallen remains of the lighthouse. The old masonry is left largely undisturbed, while newer structures have been raised around it.

These newer buildings possess a particular history of their own. When pilgrims arrive upon the island, each is given a large slab of dressed stone. The slab must be carried by the pilgrim from the point of arrival all the way to the Third Wound. They may rest along the journey and set the stone down when needed, but no other person may carry it on their behalf. Upon reaching the ruins, the stone is surrendered to the Vowed.

For generations, these stones have provided the building material from which the settlement has grown. Walls, stairs, cells, benches, cisterns, and courtyards have all been constructed from burdens carried to the site by pilgrims. Every imported stone within the monastery is therefore said to have arrived upon the back of someone walking the Red Line. The ancient rubble of the lighthouse is not used for this purpose. It remains where it fell. The pilgrims build around the ruin, but they do not rebuild it.

Once the stone has been surrendered, the second part of the rite begins. The pilgrim is given no bed, tent, lamp, or private chamber. They choose a place among the open ruins and remain there beneath the suspended lighthouse and the open sky. They may sit, lie down, sleep, pray, or meditate, but they are expected to spend the nights in stillness and attentive silence.

The ruins are rarely truly quiet. Wind passes through broken walls and shattered openings, moving through cracks in the fallen masonry and through the unreachable lighthouse above. The sea can often be heard beyond the settlement, while birds, rain, and shifting stone add their own sounds to the night. The Vowed teach that a pilgrim who enters a sufficiently deep state of stillness may begin to hear something beneath these ordinary sounds. They say it is the Hunger Beyond.

Some pilgrims describe distant breathing. Others speak of scraping, whispering, or tones that seem to come from no single direction. Some claim to have heard words they could not understand, while many hear nothing beyond the wind and the ruins. The Vowed do not promise revelation, nor do they regard silence as failure. Pilgrims are warned not to invent meaning merely because they expect to find it. The purpose of the rite is to listen, not to prove that something has spoken.

There is no prescribed number of nights a pilgrim must remain at the Unfallen Lighthouse. Some leave after their first vigil, while others remain among the ruins for several nights, returning to stillness each evening beneath the suspended tower. A pilgrim may remain for as long as they wish, and neither the Vowed nor the other pilgrims determine when their vigil is complete. The decision to leave belongs entirely to the pilgrim.

When that decision is made, they depart the ruins and continue along the Red Line. The stone they carried remains behind, eventually becoming part of the settlement that will shelter those who follow, while the broken lighthouse remains above, unreachable and unchanged.
