# The Aevra (Dragonborn)

The Aevra, known to other peoples as Dragonborn, are one of the ancient Kindreds of Ixoria. Every Aevra carries the legacy of the dragons that fell in the distant past, and every Aevra is born with a natural connection to magic. Their people are divided into seven ancestral tribes, each descended from one of the primordial lineages that emerged from the remains of Aevrenn, the Grieving. Among the Aevra, lineage is more than ancestry. It is identity, culture, and a sacred inheritance.

## Origins

The origins of the Aevra are tied directly to the Fall of the Dragons. When Drauvakh, the Fallen, was slain and the sea closed over its body, its companion Aevrenn, the Grieving, came to rest upon a mountain overlooking the waters. She waited for her companion to return, but it never did, and over a vast span of time, she too slowly died.

After her death, the mountain took her. Stone and earth gathered over her remains through the ages, until dragon and mountain could no longer be told apart. Her spine became the ridge still known as the Dragon's Spine, and her ribcage became the entrance to a vast cavern deep within the mountain, which came to be known as the Mother Cave. Ages later, fourteen primordial Aevra emerged from its depths. They appeared as seven pairs, each pair carrying one of seven ancient essences, and these fourteen are regarded as the ancestors of all Aevra, who take their name from hers. The seven primordial lineages became the seven tribes of the Aevra:

- Urrakh — Discernment
- Kharvok — Change
- Zhaurakh — Bond
- Orrakh — Dream
- Nyrakh — Distance
- Ghrauk — Instinct
- Dhaevor — Life

Some of the primordial Aevra remained among the mountain-dwelling peoples, whose own origins are traditionally traced to the Seventh Warrior of the Fall of the Dragons. Others eventually left the mountains and dispersed throughout Ixoria.

## The Seven Tribes

The seven tribes are distinct expressions of the ancient essences carried by the Aevra. Each tribe possesses its own physical characteristics, traditions, and magical affinity, and each essence finds its expression through one of the seven Spell Branches.

### Urrakh — Perception (Discernment)

The Urrakh tend to be slightly shorter than other Aevra, with compact but otherwise typical proportions, and like every Aevra they have no tail. Their scales range from deep blue to grey and overlap in fine, closely ordered rows. At a glance the rows appear uniform, yet closer study reveals subtle variations of shade and pattern that differ from one individual to the next, as though the body itself rewarded attention.

Their horns curve backward and inward, creating a natural crown-like silhouette around the head, while their eyes are unusually large and reflective, with distinctive concentric rings within the irises. Fine, feather-like scales are often scattered across the cheeks, brow, and other parts of the face, creating patterns reminiscent of freckles. They are particularly delicate compared with the larger scales covering the rest of the body.

The Urrakh have always approached magic through Perception, yet their tradition is concerned less with sight than with discernment. Among them, it is not enough to see; one must notice. They value the recognition of patterns, inconsistencies and small details that pass unremarked before other eyes, and they hold that an observation remains incomplete until meaning has been drawn from it. Two witnesses may stand before the same event, and only one may truly perceive what took place.

This outlook has shaped a people who are slow to judge and careful to observe. An Urrakh is not admired for knowing more than others, but for recognizing significance where others see only scattered details. The principle is carried in a simple saying: seeing is common; noticing is rare.

### Kharvok — Form (Change)

Kharvok scales are generally dark charcoal, brown, muted bronze, and similar subdued colors. Their most distinctive feature is the irregular distribution of those scales, which vary naturally in size and spacing across the body rather than forming consistent rows or patterns. Their horns are thick and heavily ridged, but the ridges follow random and unpredictable courses, though the horns themselves remain complete and intact, with naturally formed tips.

Many Kharvok also develop additional scale protrusions along the back, shoulders, arms, or the sides of the legs. Their size, number, and placement vary considerably from one individual to another, nor do they remain fixed: over the course of a life, protrusions may grow, wear away, or emerge where there were none before. Despite these variations, the Kharvok keep normal Aevra proportions and symmetrical, tailless bodies, and their unusual features are simply part of their natural form.

The Kharvok are drawn naturally toward Form, and they understand it through change. To them, the shape of a thing is not its truth. Size, hardness, temperature and material state are conditions that may alter while the thing itself endures.

From this has grown a deep respect for adaptability. A Kharvok who changes in answer to new circumstances is not thought weak or inconstant, provided they know which parts of themselves are fundamental and which are merely the form they happen to hold at that moment. Their bodies carry the same lesson. No two Kharvok bear quite the same scales, horns or protrusions, and irregularity among them is an ordinary condition of being rather than a flaw. Their tradition is summarized in a few words: form is temporary; substance endures.

### Zhaurakh — Connection (Bond)

The Zhaurakh are among the most visually striking of the Aevra. Their tailless bodies are covered in smooth, tightly fitted scales of rich reds, deep violets, dark golds, and almost-black tones, so naturally polished that they lend the Zhaurakh a refined appearance. Their eyes are bright and saturated in color, with distinctly narrow pupils.

Their horns are elaborate and elegant, rising from the head before curving delicately around the face rather than extending dramatically outward. Thin secondary growths branch from them and cross one another, weaving the horns into intricate, interlaced structures reminiscent of knotwork. No two Zhaurakh share the same pattern of crossings.

The Zhaurakh understand the world through Connection, and above all through the bonds that tie one thing to another. Affection is a bond, but so are loyalty, kinship, obligation, rivalry, debt, trust, hatred and betrayal. A person may be bound to another person, to a homeland, to a promise, or to something long lost that they cannot release.

The Zhaurakh therefore seldom regard anyone as an individual alone. To understand a person is to understand whom they love, what they serve, where they belong, what they owe and what they refuse to abandon. Even enmity is no absence of connection. Two enemies consumed by hatred may be bound as tightly as two devoted friends, and often for longer. At the heart of Zhaurakh thought lies a single conviction: nothing exists entirely alone, and to understand a thing, one must understand what it is bound to.

### Orrakh — Manifestation (Dream)

The Orrakh are generally lanky and slender compared with other Aevra, and like their kin they have no tail. Their scales range through pale silver, muted lavender, deep indigo, and blue-grey, and are so fine and delicate that their bodies take on a soft and almost unreal appearance. Some scales are partially translucent, particularly about the face and neck, and possess a subtle iridescence reminiscent of the inner surface of a seashell, catching and shifting the light as the Aevra moves.

Their horns curve upward in branching forms reminiscent of antlers, with branching patterns that vary between individuals. Their eyes are extremely dark, often appearing almost entirely black, with little visible distinction between iris and pupil.

The Orrakh are associated with Manifestation, which they understand through the idea of the dream. A dream, in their tradition, is not confined to sleep. It is anything that exists before it has physical reality: an image, an idea, a story, a possibility, something imagined and not yet brought into the world. Manifestation is that principle made visible, for through it, what did not exist may be given presence, however briefly.

The Orrakh consequently hold imagination in particular esteem. Artistry, invention, storytelling and unconventional thought all carry the same principle within them, since nothing new can be made until it has first been conceived. To many outsiders, the Orrakh themselves, with their fine, half-translucent scales, seem only partly given to the world. Their tradition holds that everything created once existed as something unreal.

### Nyrakh — Space (Distance)

The Nyrakh can possess almost any base scale color, and they are recognized instead by the pattern that covers their tailless bodies. Long bands narrow and converge as they run along the body, much as a road or a river appears to narrow as it recedes toward the horizon. Their colors contrast strongly with the underlying scales, so that a pale Nyrakh may carry deep black bands while a dark-scaled individual might carry almost white ones. Seen from certain angles, the converging lines lend the body a curious impression of depth, and they remain one of the clearest indicators of Nyrakh ancestry.

Their horns are long and slender, sweeping backward from the brow in a smooth, unbroken line. Their eyes are pale and clear, with irises so faint that they are easily overlooked, and even when a Nyrakh looks directly at someone, their gaze often seems settled upon something farther away.

The Nyrakh possess a natural relationship with Space, which they understand chiefly through distance. Their traditions dwell on the difference between here and there, near and far, departure and arrival. Distance, to them, is not a fixed property of the world but a relationship between places, and like any relationship, it may be altered.

Travel, boundaries, separation and return consequently carry unusual weight among the Nyrakh. No place is unreachable merely because it is distant, and two locations that seem impossibly far apart may yet be brought together. Separation is held less absolutely among them than among most peoples. Here and there, they say, are divided only by what lies between them.

### Ghrauk — Force (Instinct)

The Ghrauk are generally the most muscular and physically bulky of the Aevra tribes. Their bodies have a distinctly predatory appearance, though their proportions remain recognizably Aevra, and like all their kin they are without a tail. Their scales are typically earthy reds, ochres, dark greens, browns, and near-black tones, and are large, thick, and irregular.

In some areas of the body, these scales gather into particularly large concentrations that appear almost like natural armor. Such formations are especially common around the shoulders, chest, knees, and other vulnerable areas, though their exact arrangement varies from individual to individual. Their horns grow sideways before curving forward, and together with their somewhat more pronounced jaws and teeth, they give the Ghrauk a powerful, predatory silhouette that reflects the physicality of their lineage.

The Ghrauk are inclined by nature toward Force, which their traditions associate with instinct: the immediacy of movement, reaction and momentum, the capacity to answer circumstance before conscious thought has caught up with it. This is not mere impulse. What the Ghrauk prize is trained instinct, the point at which experience has been absorbed so deeply that action no longer waits on deliberation. A practiced warrior moves before naming the danger; an experienced hunter turns toward a movement before knowing its cause.

Force suits this outlook naturally, for movement may be begun, redirected, hastened or brought to a sudden halt, and what matters is recognizing the moment that demands action and committing to it. Among the Ghrauk it is said that thought may understand an action afterward, but instinct knows when to act.

### Dhaevor — Vitality (Life)

The Dhaevor are generally taller than other Aevra, often possessing a noticeably greater stature while retaining otherwise natural, tailless proportions. Their scales come in vibrant greens, warm golds, copper, turquoise, and deep natural reds, and have a layered appearance, with smaller scales naturally developing between larger ones. This gives their bodies an impression of continuous growth and renewal.

Their horns grow upward and develop subtle branching structures reminiscent of roots or antlers, the degree of branching varying between individuals. Their eyes are bright and often appear almost luminous, with colors that contrast strongly against their scales.

The Dhaevor are associated with Vitality, and their ancestral understanding of it is simply life. Life, to them, means far more than healing. It encompasses the whole condition of living things: birth, growth, strength, sickness, injury, recovery, age, withering and death.

The Dhaevor regard life as a continuing process rather than a settled state. Growth and decline are two movements of the same cycle; the forces that allow a body to heal are kin to those through which it weakens, and flourishing and fading are different expressions of a single vitality. Their own bodies seem to bear witness to this, with layered scales that suggest constant renewal and horns that branch like roots. Their tradition teaches that to understand life, one must understand both what makes it flourish and what makes it fade.

## Aevra and Magic

All Aevra are natural magic wielders. Magic is not something they must learn before they can use it; it is an inherent part of their lineage. Each pure-lineage Aevra is born with a natural affinity for the Spell Branch through which the essence of their tribe is expressed. The Urrakh are drawn to Perception, the Kharvok to Form, the Zhaurakh to Connection, the Orrakh to Manifestation, the Nyrakh to Space, the Ghrauk to Force, and the Dhaevor to Vitality.

Their magical manifestations are expressions of these essences. An Aevra does not simply study an abstract school of magic, for their magic emerges from something already present within them. The exact ways in which an essence manifests can vary between individuals, but the ancestral affinity remains constant within a pure lineage.

## The Vayra

The Vayra are Aevra born from parents belonging to two different tribes. Among the seven tribes, lineage is considered sacred, and each tribe takes great pride in its ancestry and in the purity of its connection to one of the seven primordial essences. For this reason, unions between members of different tribes are strongly frowned upon.

A child born from such a union does not possess the full essence of either parent. Instead, both ancestral essences are present within them in an incomplete form. One essence may manifest more strongly than the other, and this balance can differ from one Vayra to another. Their physical characteristics may likewise reflect both lineages, with scale colors, patterns, horn shapes, eye characteristics, and other traits mingling in ways that vary between individuals.

Their magic is similarly uncertain. A Vayra does not possess the full magical expression of either parent, yet the two incomplete essences may interact in ways that are not fully understood, and there is no established pattern for what such interactions can produce.

The Vayra are shunned, but they are not hunted. They are not required to remain within either parent's tribe and are generally expected to leave and live their lives elsewhere. There is no eighth Vayra tribe.

## Culture and Lineage

For the Aevra, lineage is inseparable from identity. To belong to a tribe is to carry the legacy of one of the seven primordial lineages and the essence associated with it. This pride shapes much of Aevra society: their tribes maintain distinct identities and traditions, and the purity of one's lineage is treated with great importance.

The Vayra therefore occupy an uncomfortable place in Aevra culture. They carry the blood of two tribes but are regarded as belonging fully to neither. While they are not necessarily treated with violence, their exclusion from tribal life is an accepted part of their existence.

## The Vigil of the Returning

When an Aevra comes of age, they undertake a solitary pilgrimage to the Mother Cave, the ancient cavern whose entrance is formed by the ribcage of Aevrenn. They enter alone, carrying only a few essential supplies. No companion may accompany them, and no member of their tribe may guide them inside.

The Mother Cave is vast and completely dark. Nobody has ever properly mapped its interior, and its passages extend deep into the mountain. Once inside, the Aevra must rely on their own powers, instincts, and will to find their way through the darkness, until they find a place where they can sit and wait. They do not know how long they will remain there.

At some point, each Aevra receives a personal signal telling them that their Vigil is complete. The nature of this signal is different for every individual: it may be a sound, a vision, a sensation, a change in the darkness, or something else entirely, and no two Aevra necessarily experience the same thing. Once they receive it, they must rise and find their own way back out of the Mother Cave.

Most return safely, but some do not. Disappearances are extremely rare, yet they are accepted as an unavoidable part of the Vigil, and those who never emerge are not considered failures. Among the Aevra, it is said that they have returned to the Mother. The Vayra alone are forbidden from entering the Mother Cave and may never undertake the Vigil.
