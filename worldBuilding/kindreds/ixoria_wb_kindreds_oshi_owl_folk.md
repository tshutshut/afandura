# The Oshi

## The Quiet Kindred

The Oshi are an owl-like Kindred known throughout Ixoria for their intelligence, reserve, and peculiar manner. They are attentive listeners and careful observers, often allowing long silences to settle before offering an answer. Even then, their responses can be indirect, proverbial, or strangely constructed, leaving others uncertain whether the Oshi has answered the question at all.

Their appearance and movements contribute greatly to the unease they sometimes inspire. Their large eyes remain remarkably attentive, while their heads can turn suddenly and at angles that other Kindreds find unnatural. More unsettling still is their quietness. An Oshi can enter a room, cross a library, or approach a conversation with remarkably little sound, occasionally startling those who simply did not notice their arrival.

None of this is generally deliberate. The Oshi do not cultivate an eerie reputation, and many appear genuinely puzzled by the discomfort their ordinary behavior can cause. As a result, their relationship with most other Kindreds is characterized less by hostility than by a certain distance. They are respected and frequently welcomed, but seldom entirely understood.

## Scriptum Vesperum

At the center of Oshi society stands an ancient and widespread institution formally known as the Scriptum Vesperum. Across Ixoria, it is far more commonly called the Order of the Quill, or simply the Order.

The Order concerns itself with the preservation, restoration, reproduction, and circulation of knowledge. Its members seek positions wherever written information is collected or maintained, and Oshi can consequently be found serving as librarians, archivists, scribes, copyists, printers, translators, historians, restorers, and scholars. Their interest in learning also brings many of them into institutions belonging to other Kindreds. Oshi scholars and scribes, for example, are a familiar presence within the halls and collections of Orden College.

The Order considers the preservation of knowledge a service rather than merely an academic pursuit. Its members restore damaged manuscripts, reproduce fragile books, organize neglected collections, preserve civic and historical records, and assist those searching for works that would otherwise be difficult to obtain. Where circumstances permit, books are lent or copies commissioned and transported between distant collections.

This work can extend considerably beyond the bookshelves themselves. The Order has been known to finance the restoration of old archives and libraries, sponsor scholarly work, and contribute toward public projects intended to preserve or disseminate knowledge. Consequently, even those who find the Oshi unsettling tend to regard the Order of the Quill favorably. Its members have spent generations making themselves useful wherever knowledge is in danger of being forgotten.

## The Houses of the Order

The Scriptum Vesperum maintains houses throughout Ixoria, particularly in major cities and important centers of learning. Their scale varies enormously. A modest house might consist of little more than a collection of books, a scriptorium, and rooms for several members of the Order, while the greatest maintain extensive archives, workshops, reading rooms, collections, and accommodations for visiting scholars.

Together, these houses form a network through which information can travel. An individual seeking an uncommon book may approach the local Order and discover that no copy exists there, only for a request to be sent elsewhere in Ixoria. The Order makes no promise that every work can be found or provided, but its members have a reputation for pursuing such requests with extraordinary patience.

This network also explains why the Oshi are so widely dispersed. They need not remain concentrated within any single homeland or settlement. Wherever knowledge accumulates, there is reason for an Oshi to be present.

For most inhabitants of Ixoria, this is the defining contribution of the Kindred. Empires change, institutions disappear, buildings burn, and private collections are abandoned, but the Oshi arrive with ink, paper, careful hands, and considerable patience.

## The Empty Chair

Perhaps the strangest publicly known Oshi custom is also one of the simplest.

Whenever a group of Oshi gathers somewhere that requires seating, they prepare one additional chair. This happens during meals, meetings, scholarly discussions, formal gatherings, and even relatively ordinary social occasions. If twelve Oshi are expected, thirteen chairs are arranged.

The additional chair remains empty.

Outsiders have naturally asked about the custom, but Oshi explanations are characteristically unsatisfying. One might say that the chair has been left for a guest who has yet to arrive. Another may explain that it is courteous to remember those who have not yet been accounted for. A third may simply remark that one should never presume to know everyone who will attend.

Attempts to press the matter rarely produce anything clearer.

No mysterious guest appears. Nobody eventually takes the empty place, and the Oshi themselves behave as though there is nothing unusual about it. When the gathering concludes, the unused chair is simply put away with all the others.

Those who spend enough time around the Oshi eventually learn not to ask.

There is always one more chair.
