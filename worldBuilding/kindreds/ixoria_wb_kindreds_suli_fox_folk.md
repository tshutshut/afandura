# The Suli (Fox Folk)

The Suli's oldest communities lie within the forests of Ixoria. Their ancestral dens are still thriving, though many Suli eventually leave them for villages, cities, ports and roads, drawn by trade and opportunity. They are clever, adaptable and rarely indifferent to profit, and wherever they go, they carry with them the craft of the den that raised them.

## The Four Families

The Suli have long been divided into four great families, each with a craft passed down through generations. The Suli-Lei are skilled in chemistry, producing compounds and potions from natural materials whose effects may be useful, harmful or simply unusual. The Suli-Ru are known for pyrotechnics, making fireworks, smoke bombs, sparks and small explosives, and often turn their craft into a profitable trade. The Suli-Thi are masters of forgery, able to reproduce documents, artworks, instruments and valuables closely enough to deceive all but the most discerning eye. The Suli-Kal devote themselves to games, gambling, wagers and contests of both chance and strategy.

In the old days, a Suli's fur told you their family at a glance: silver for the Suli-Lei, red for the Suli-Ru, white for the Suli-Thi and black for the Suli-Kal. Generations of marriages between the families have blurred those lines, and children of mixed families are accepted without distinction. Today a Suli's coat hints at their ancestry more than it decides where they belong.

What matters is the den. A Suli may live in whichever den they choose, learn its craft and take its loyalties as their own, and once settled there, their strongest loyalty belongs to that den rather than to the family they came from. Among the Suli, where one belongs matters more than where one began. The families compete fiercely and are not above sabotaging one another, yet their rivalry ends where the interests of the Suli as a whole begin.

## The Dens

Each family keeps not one den but many, scattered through the forests of Ixoria. Some are linked to one another by hidden tunnels and paths, while others stand alone, far from any other. Outsiders may know the forest, and even the general region where a den lies, but finding the den itself is another matter. False entrances, concealed paths, misleading trails and chambers hidden behind ordinary woodland have protected these places for generations.

The location of a true den is among the most closely guarded secrets of the Suli. Families may know one another's secrets and occasionally boast of it, but no Suli, whatever their family or birthplace, willingly reveals a true den to an outsider. On the rare occasions an outsider has found one, it has never ended well.

## Foxes' Foolery

In spring comes Foxes' Foolery, a festival during which every Suli knows that another family, den or individual is likely preparing some elaborate trick, theft, wager or scheme. The exact nature of the deception is never known in advance, so businesses become guarded, valuables disappear into safer places, and everyone watches their neighbours a little more carefully than usual. The schemes may involve fortunes or reputations, but serious injury and death are not the point. The pleasure lies in the cleverness of the attempt, the quality of the deception and the satisfaction of discovering how it was done.

Among the Suli, rivalry is expected, suspicion is useful, and trust is rarely given without reason. Yet when an outsider threatens them, the old divisions become secondary, and they have a saying for it: "Argue among foxes, but hunt the wolf together."

## The Forged Feast

For all their dealings with the wider world, the Suli remain wary of outsiders, and that caution turns into something harsher when Wolfkin are involved. The hostility between the two peoples is old, visceral and rarely concealed, and it began with a dispute over land.

During the reign of Alaric the Stern, the Suli and the Wolfkin quarrelled over territory and over access to forests and hunting grounds. The matter was brought before the Beacon, who settled it in favour of the Wolfkin. The Suli protested, but Alaric was not a ruler who changed his mind. The Suli accepted the ruling in appearance only. If the Wolfkin had won the land, the Suli resolved, they would come to regret it, and the four families joined in a single scheme, something they rarely do on such a scale.

The Suli-Thi began with an invitation. It came, apparently, from local notables wishing to celebrate the settlement with a great banquet in honour of the Wolfkin, and it was only the first of many forgeries. Invitations went out to guests of modest standing, chosen carefully enough that none of them would draw suspicion, and orders for food and drink went out to caterers, cooks and servers. The whole event, from the guest list to the last cask, was forged, and yet every part of it was real enough that no one thought to doubt it. The Wolfkin were wary, but they attended, bringing a strong guard of their own warriors.

The banquet's chief drink was to be a mead brewed in the homeland of the Borru. On the road to the feast, the Borru merchant carrying it stopped for the night at a tavern, where the Suli-Kal were waiting for him. They kept him at the tables all night with wager after wager, and they let him win. By the time he and his companions fell asleep, happy and well drunk, the casks had been spiked with a potion prepared by the Suli-Lei. The merchant went on his way the next morning, knowing nothing.

The potion was meant to make the Wolfkin drunk, playful and quarrelsome, so that they would make fools of themselves before the guests. The Suli-Lei were masters of their craft, but they had never tested it on Wolfkin, and they had assumed it would act on them as it acted on the Suli. It did not. As the banquet went on, the Wolfkin grew more and more aggressive, until the Suli-Ru began their part. Fireworks rose all around the clearing, meant only to startle the guests of honour, and their bursts took the shapes of forest spirits and monsters. To the frenzied Wolfkin, it seemed they were surrounded by enemies. They turned on everything around them, guests, servers and one another alike. By the time they came to their senses, the clearing was full of the dead, Wolfkin and many others from almost every Kindred.

Still enraged, the Wolfkin learned how they had been deceived, and they tracked the Suli-Ru who had fled the clearing. The trail led them to an isolated true den. No Suli in it survived, and more Wolfkin fell before the killing was done. For the Suli, it was a tragedy twice over, for their own had died and a true den had been found. What followed was open war between the two peoples, remembered as the War of Fang and Claw. The war ended under the Beacon, yet it never ended between the Suli and the Wolfkin themselves.
