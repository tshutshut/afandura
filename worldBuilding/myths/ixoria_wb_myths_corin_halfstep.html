Corin Halfstep

Among the countless stories born during the fall of the Aurelate, few have endured as long as the tale of Corin Halfstep. Whether true, embellished, or entirely imagined, none can say. Yet travelers still speak his name when the roads fall quiet after sunset.

During the single year of Aster the Broken's reign, the Veilsteps did not fail all at once. They faded without warning. At times, a single Veilstep vanished. At others, several failed within days. Trade faltered, settlements became isolated, and fear spread throughout the Aurelate.

It was during this time that a traveler named Corin claimed to hear the whispers of the Veilsteps. He never called himself a prophet. Instead, he maintained that the gateways remembered every path they had ever connected. Several of his predictions concerning which Veilsteps would survive proved correct, and in time he gathered devoted followers.

Corin preached while standing within a Veilstep, with one half of his body in one settlement and the other half in another. Every sermon began with the same words:

"The Veilstep remembers this path."

He taught that only worthy paths would endure.

One day, as Corin began another sermon, he repeated his familiar opening:

"The Veilstep remembers this path."

Before he could continue, the Veilstep faded. The ancient gateway vanished without warning, and Corin was split perfectly in half from head to foot. He died instantly.

Some called it divine punishment. Others called it cruel chance. From that day onward, he became known only as Corin Halfstep.

Legend holds that Corin's soul was divided as his body had been. Unable to become whole, neither half found peace.

Travelers claim to encounter one half of him wandering lonely roads, hopping upon a single leg through forests, mountain passes, lonely harbors, and forgotten tracks. Every witness agrees on one thing: the apparition asks only:

"Have you seen the other me?"

Some say the wandering half is gentle. Others say it follows travelers for miles. Some believe that ignoring it causes it to lose interest, while others insist it nods quietly when answered truthfully before continuing its endless search.

No one has ever reported seeing both halves together.

The old road rhyme is still known in some places:

Halfstep asks where Halfstep's gone,
Answer true... then travel on.
