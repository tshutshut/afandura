# WB - Kindreds - Nyr

## The Nyr

Known to outsiders simply as the **Ravenfolk** or **Crowfolk**, the Nyr have no homeland, kingdom, or central realm. They are scattered throughout the continent, living and working wherever their particular skills are needed. They gather in small groups known as **Nyriad**, but no central government or authority appears to unite them.

The Nyr are almost always encountered beneath layers of black, translucent clothing. Their hoods conceal most of their faces, leaving only their beaks visible, while their wings remain folded behind their backs beneath the cloth. This gives them an unusual, hunched silhouette when they walk. Despite being capable of flight, Nyr generally keep their wings concealed and walk when moving through settlements.

Their speech is as distinctive as their appearance. Like real crows and ravens, the Nyr can reproduce human speech, but their voices retain a harsh, rasping quality. Even ordinary conversation can therefore sound strange or unsettling to outsiders.

## The Nyriad and the Bells

When a Nyriad travels through a city, village, or along a road, the leading Nyr carries a staff decorated with several small bells of different pitches. The bells are rung rhythmically as the group travels. Other Nyr possess similar staffs, although no two are exactly alike.

The sound has become widely recognizable. People often know that a Nyriad is approaching before they can see them, and will make way or prepare themselves for whatever work has brought the Nyr there.

## The Work Nobody Wants

The Nyr are known for performing the foul, unpleasant, dangerous, or otherwise unwanted work that other people would rather avoid. Their services are nevertheless often essential.

They may be called upon to collect corpses, investigate or identify diseases, deal with dangerous remains, perform shamanic or spiritual work, or undertake other tasks that ordinary communities are reluctant to approach. Their reputation is therefore built upon a contradiction: people need the Nyr, yet many remain uneasy in their presence.

## The Royal Nyr

The Nyr have no known central government, and no ordinary system of centralized leadership has been identified among them. Nevertheless, there are persistent accounts of mysterious **royal Nyr** or other central figures who are almost never encountered by outsiders.

Their true nature, authority, organization, and relationship with ordinary Nyr remain unknown.

## The Veil Steps

The Nyr are unique among the known peoples of the realm in having categorically refused to use the **Veil Steps**.

When Aurelia activated the Veil Steps and began using them to unite the Aurelate, the Nyr had access to the network but deliberately refused to travel through it. They continued to travel by traditional means: on foot, by wing, by boat, and by other conventional forms of transportation.

Their refusal was not a matter of lacking access. They simply would not use the Veil Steps.

The Nyr have never provided outsiders with a satisfactory explanation. Even during the Aurelate, when the Veil Steps formed the foundation of travel and communication across the realm, the Nyr continued to avoid them. Once the Veil Steps began to fail, their refusal ceased to have practical significance, but the mystery surrounding it remained.

## The Birds of the Nyr

The Nyr have developed unusual relationships with several species of ordinary birds. These relationships are not necessarily mystical. Over generations, the Nyr have fed, protected, and cared for particular birds while learning to interpret their movements, alarm calls, and changes in behavior.

The birds therefore function as a living network of information. Their presence can help Nyr notice unusual activity, movement, danger, or disturbances in places where no Nyr is present. This is one possible explanation for the longstanding observation that a Nyriad can sometimes appear at a place where something unusual has happened before anyone appears to have summoned them.

It is not uncommon for flocks of birds to approach a newly arrived Nyriad. The Nyr may feed them, allow them to gather around their camps, or tend to them in other ways. Outsiders sometimes interpret these encounters as evidence of something supernatural. The Nyr themselves have little reason to correct such assumptions.

### The Murrik

The Nyr have an almost universal disdain for pigeons, or **Murrik**. Their objection is not simply that pigeons are unintelligent. Rather, the Nyr consider them terrible informants.

Pigeons witness enormous amounts of human activity, but appear incapable of distinguishing what matters from what does not. A Murrik may witness a murder, a wedding, a merchant caravan, and someone dropping a piece of bread, yet offer no useful distinction between any of them. Their opportunistic nature only reinforces the Nyr's distrust.

The Nyr therefore consider pigeons a source of noise rather than useful information. The sentiment is unusual precisely because the Nyr otherwise maintain such close relationships with birds.

## The Night of Bells

Every sixty-six days, the Nyr observe a night known simply as the **Night of Bells**. It is one of the few traditions shared by Nyr across the continent, despite the absence of any central authority or common homeland. On that night, the Nyr leave their dwellings and gradually converge upon a place they have designated within their settlement as the gathering place. It need not be a central square, nor is it necessarily a place of importance to the people who live there. It belongs to the Nyr because the Nyr have chosen it.

As the night progresses, Nyr emerge from alleys, houses, workshops, and hidden quarters, all dressed in their familiar black shrouds. They gather until the designated place becomes a dense, almost motionless mass of black figures. From a distance, the gathering can appear less like a crowd of individuals than a single dark shape occupying the square.

At the appointed moment, the bells begin. Hundreds of small bells of different pitches ring together, creating a strange, layered sound that carries through the surrounding streets. The Nyr then raise their voices and crow together, their harsh calls joining the bells for several minutes. Nobody outside the gathering is expected to participate, and it is common knowledge that the Nyr should be left undisturbed while the Black Mass takes place.

When the bells finally fall silent, the Nyr disperse as quietly as they arrived. By morning, the gathering place has returned to being an ordinary part of the city, and the Night of Bells becomes another sixty-six days away.

## The Great Migration

The Nyr are accustomed to movement, but their migrations do not follow a calendar that outsiders can understand. Over periods of several years, a city may gradually lose much of its Nyr population. There is no announcement, procession, or single moment of departure. One Nyriad leaves, then another, and eventually more begin to follow. Some travel toward another city, some disappear along the roads, and others simply cease to be seen in the places where they once worked.

Nobody knows exactly when a Great Migration will begin, how long it will last, or why the Nyr choose to move. The intervals between migrations vary, and the Nyr themselves rarely offer explanations. To an outsider, it can seem as though an invisible tide has slowly changed direction.

Not everyone leaves. Some Nyr remain behind, sometimes for decades, while others gradually disappear from a city that had once seemed to contain a permanent Nyriad presence. Eventually, another city may begin to experience the opposite: Nyr arriving in increasing numbers, settling into familiar streets and establishing themselves where others have departed.

The movement is therefore neither an exodus nor a journey toward a single destination. It is a slow redistribution of the Nyr across the continent, occurring according to a rhythm that appears to belong to them alone.

## The Passing of the Black Mantle

Every city with a longstanding Nyr presence eventually has its **Nyrath**. The Nyrath is a veteran Nyr who has remained in the city for many years, sometimes decades, and has accumulated an extraordinary body of knowledge about the place: its people, its streets, its hidden corners, its dangers, its customs, and the countless things that a Nyr may be called upon to deal with.

The position is not one of conventional rulership. The Nyrath does not command every Nyr in the city, nor does the title appear to be formally bestowed by any central authority. Instead, it represents **standing, memory, and accumulated knowledge**. A Nyrath is the one who has been there long enough to remember what others have forgotten.

When the Nyrath decides that their time has come to leave, they choose their successor from within their own Nyriad. The choice is theirs alone. The departing Nyrath passes on what they know, along with the standing and responsibilities that accompany the position. Once this passage is complete, the former Nyrath simply leaves.

Nobody outside the Nyr truly knows where such Nyr go. Some are said to join the great migrations. Others disappear onto the roads and are never seen again. The Nyr do not appear troubled by this uncertainty. To them, the departure is not an ending, but another passage: **knowledge remains, the position remains, and the Nyrath moves on.**
