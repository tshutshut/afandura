# The Morowan Revolt

## Morowa and the Harbour

The Morowan Revolt began not with an army at the gates, but with years of hardship along the docks of Morowa. Among the Aurelate's most important coastal cities, Morowa occupied a valuable position on the routes connecting Ixoria's settlements and ports. Its ships also crossed the seas to trade with lands beyond Ixoria, whose affairs remained distant from those of the empire. The city's harbour was consequently more than a source of local wealth. It was a strategic passage for goods and supplies that the Aurelate could ill afford to lose.

Much of Morowa's commercial life lay in the hands of the Morowan Shipping Syndicate, whose influence extended over shipping, warehouses, cargo handling and the labour that sustained them. The city was governed by Arven the Fourth, a Lionfolk ruler whose family had held its leading position for four generations. Under Arven and his forebears, merchants, shipowners and prominent guilds had prospered. Yet the prosperity of the upper quarters stood in growing contrast to the conditions of those who worked below. The wealth passing through Morowa was considerable; the share reaching its labourers was not.

Tieflings formed a substantial part of the harbour workforce. Already subject to suspicion and prejudice elsewhere in Ixoria, many had travelled to Morowa in search of steady employment and a place to settle. The Syndicate readily employed them, but offered meagre wages, long and dangerous shifts, and humble accommodation in crowded quarters near the docks. Their uncertain standing made protest difficult, while the constant arrival of ships and workers allowed the system to endure. Over the years, the gap between those who governed the harbour and those who kept it operating widened.

## Meredis Long-Standing

Among those who sought a living in Morowa was Meredis, who had originally been an elf. She was already an adult when, without any known cause, she underwent the permanent transformation that made her a Tiefling. She left her former home and travelled from place to place before arriving in Morowa, where she hoped at last to establish a settled life. She found work on the docks and shared the difficult circumstances of the people around her.

Other Tieflings eventually gave her the second name Long-Standing. Meredis was spirited, outspoken and often unwilling to let an injustice pass without protest. She challenged overseers, intervened when workers were mistreated, and spoke for those afraid of losing their employment. Her standing among the labourers grew without appointment or ceremony. She became a person to whom others turned because she had repeatedly stood beside them.

## The City Unravels

During Nyra the Tempest's reign as the ninth Beacon, the Aurelate was passing through increasingly difficult times. Its demands upon its territories grew, and Morowa's strategic harbour made it an obvious place from which to seek more. Nyra ordered the city to increase the movement of goods and shipping, to turn vessels around more quickly and to sustain a more relentless pace of trade in service of the empire. The Syndicate and Morowa's ruling establishment complied by transferring the burden to their workforce. Hours lengthened, the pace intensified, and living and working conditions that had long been poor deteriorated further. Those who benefited most from the harbour continued to prosper, while the people handling its cargo were asked to give more for little in return.

Discontent spread through the docks and then beyond them. The unrest did not overturn Morowa in a single day. Over several weeks, demonstrations became strikes, confrontations and riots. Arven's government attempted to restore order, first through its existing authority and then by deploying the city's order keepers into the affected districts. Barricades were removed, crowds dispersed and streets briefly reclaimed, only for resistance to appear elsewhere. A neighbourhood secured one day might be lost the next. Dockworkers found support among their families, neighbours and other inhabitants who had grievances against the city's rulers. Little by little, the authorities lost their hold on the lower city.

As the struggle continued, its demands changed. Better wages, tolerable hours and decent housing had once seemed possible goals, but Meredis and those who followed her no longer believed the Syndicate and the existing government would protect them. They demanded that the established ruling body be removed and that a new government take control of Morowa, one committed to improving conditions for the population as a whole. Their aim was not simply to replace one privileged group with another. They wanted to break the political order that had sustained the exploitation. Although Morowa already enjoyed considerable freedom in its daily affairs under the Aurelate, the rebels' claim to govern themselves made their actions appear perilously close to secession.

Word eventually reached Nyra that the city's own authorities could no longer contain the uprising. The danger was both practical and political. Morowa was too important a harbour to remain outside effective imperial control, and allowing its recognised rulers to be overthrown by force could encourage other cities to attempt the same. Nyra therefore led the intervention herself. A settlement was still briefly possible: she was prepared to entertain improvements in the workers' conditions, but insisted that the existing political authority be restored. Meredis and her supporters heard in this an invitation to return to the very system they had risen against. They refused, and Nyra's willingness to negotiate ended.

## The Retaking of Morowa

The arrival of the Aurelate's regular army changed the conflict. Where Morowa's order keepers had struggled for weeks against protests and neighbourhood resistance, Nyra commanded trained soldiers, officers and mages equipped for sustained fighting. The rebels resisted with barricades, improvised weapons and whatever positions they could defend among the streets, workshops, warehouses and dockyards. Yet they could not withstand the army's organised advance. Within a few days, the imperial forces retook the lower city block by block, driving the uprising from the districts where it had begun.

Meredis and the main body of the remaining rebels withdrew to Keren Var, an ancient citadel standing on a narrow, elevated rocky height above Morowa, with little room beyond its fortifications and a few surrounding buildings. The citadel predated the city itself and had been built by dwarves in an earlier age. Its walls had endured long after the original purpose of the stronghold had faded from memory. Access was ordinarily provided by a wooden bridge spanning the gap between the citadel's height and the neighbouring cliff.

The rebels took hostages with them: Arven the Fourth, members of his entourage, prominent merchants, guild masters and other representatives of the order they sought to replace. These captives embodied the system against which the uprising had begun, but they also gave the retreating rebels leverage. Once Meredis and her followers had crossed into Keren Var, they destroyed the wooden bridge behind them. Nyra had recovered the harbour and the streets below, but the rebels and Morowa's recognised rulers remained beyond her reach.

A patient commander might have surrounded the citadel and waited for hunger or exhaustion to compel surrender. Nyra would not. Her temperament had little room for a prolonged stalemate, and she considered the recovery of the hostages a matter of imperial authority. Arven and the others had governed with the Aurelate's recognition; leaving them to perish in rebel hands would suggest that the empire could reclaim a city while abandoning those who ruled it in its name. Nyra resolved to storm Keren Var, and to do so she needed a new crossing.

## Hedera Durrn and the Bridge

The Beacon summoned Hedera Durrn, a respected dwarven buildmaster. The request carried an irony Hedera understood better than most. Before the rise of the Aurelate and its Veil Steps, dwarven builders had been renowned for the roads, bridges, harbours and great structures through which they connected settlements. They were an outward-looking people whose pride and honour rested in the endurance and quality of what they made. Over the generations of the Beacon Dynasty, travel through the Veil Steps had reduced the need for many of those works. Dwarves had not been abandoned or reduced to poverty, but commissions, wealth, prestige and political standing had diminished. Their skill remained, while the empire increasingly found less occasion to call upon it.

Hedera had lived through that loss of standing. Now the ninth Beacon, after generations of diminished reliance on dwarven work, demanded a bridge to attack a citadel that dwarves themselves had built. Hedera sympathised with the exploited Tieflings and was troubled by the use to which her work would be put. She did not necessarily approve of the taking of hostages, but understood the grievances that had driven the uprising. She also knew Nyra's reputation and feared that refusal might bring reprisals upon dwarven communities already in a delicate position. The Beacon's summons left her little genuine choice. Hedera reluctantly accepted, and with a company of fellow dwarven builders set about constructing a narrow crossing of timber, iron and carefully placed supports, capable of carrying soldiers and horses across the gulf under the threat of battle.

The bridge was completed rapidly. It was a difficult work, raised in dangerous circumstances, and its narrow passage allowed only a limited number of soldiers to cross abreast. Hedera had fulfilled her duty as a builder, even while fearing the consequences of its success.

## The Assault at Dusk

The final assault began at dusk. From the ground near the completed bridge, where Hedera stood with her dwarven companions, Keren Var rose against the last light. The lower city was already falling into shadow. A hard wind came from the sea, pulling at cloaks and banners and carrying the smells of salt, smoke and burned timber. Soldiers crowded the approach to the bridge, their shields knocking together as officers formed the ranks. Horses stamped upon the earth, and spearheads and blades caught the fading light.

Nyra rode before her army upon an enormous, broad-chested warhorse, muscular and powerful enough to match its rider. She was a strong, broad-shouldered woman clad in armour, her head uncovered to the wind. In one hand she held the reins; in the other, a great battle-axe. Her soldiers watched her pass, and then the first weapon struck a shield. Others answered in a measured, two-beat rhythm, calling her name: “Tem-pest! Tem-pest!” The chant spread through the assembled ranks. Swords and spears rose, steel hammered against shields, and hundreds of voices repeated the name until it rolled across the height and reached the dwarves standing farther back.

Nyra raised her axe, and the noise subsided. With Keren Var before her and the wind crossing the assembled army, she delivered the words that soldiers would later repeat:

> They were given the chance to yield. They were given the chance to return to the Aurelate, and they answered us with steel.
>
> Let no city believe it may deny the Beacon and remain beneath her protection. Let no rebel mistake patience for weakness.
>
> I am Nyra the Tempest. I will not send you where I will not go myself.

The army erupted in cheers. Weapons struck shields and soldiers shouted her name, their voices rising until the sound seemed to fill the entire approach. Nyra turned her horse toward the citadel. As the clamour diminished, she looked across Hedera's bridge toward the defenders waiting beyond it. “They would not kneel,” she said. For a brief moment there was only the wind. Then she raised her battle-axe toward Keren Var and declared, “Then let them be buried standing.”

The soldiers answered with a final roar. The chant of “Tem-pest! Tem-pest!” rose again as Nyra spurred her horse onto the bridge. Hooves thundered over the new timbers, followed by the boots and armour of the first ranks. The crossing trembled beneath the charge, but held. Nyra led her soldiers into the citadel, and the ranks continued across until the last of them had disappeared within the ancient walls. Hedera remained behind with the dwarves who had built the bridge, watching the army vanish into the fortress her craft had made accessible.

## The Fall of Keren Var

For a time, those outside could distinguish the sounds of fighting within the citadel. Gates crashed, steel struck steel, and orders and screams carried unevenly upon the wind. Soon the separate noises merged into the continuous sound of battle. Flames appeared in one part of the fortress and then another. Brief flashes of spellwork illuminated windows and battlements, sometimes white, sometimes red or violet, each followed by a distant crack or concussion. The fighting continued, while Hedera and the others outside could do nothing but watch and listen.

Then a single, piercing green flash burst from deep within Keren Var. For an instant, the walls, towers and battlements stood out in unnatural light against the gathering dark. A violent blast followed. The air struck those watching from outside, the ground shifted beneath their feet, timber groaned, and horses screamed. Hedera felt the shock in her chest as the height trembled. One tower folded inward, a wall split from top to bottom, and sections of the old citadel began falling into one another. Stone crashed through stone until much of Keren Var disappeared beneath a rolling cloud of dust.

No one outside the fortress knew what had caused the destruction. In later accounts, a rumour arose that a Tiefling warlock had been among the defenders, his powers having manifested long before the revolt. According to this account, he had learned to exercise some control over his magic, but warlock magic was bound to emotion. Amid the terror, anger and desperation of the final battle, his control was said to have failed, releasing a last spell with a force far beyond his intention. Whether such a warlock was present, or whether his magic caused the catastrophe, remains uncertain. No witness survived inside the citadel to establish what occurred, and those beyond its walls knew only the green flash, the shock wave and the collapse.

Nyra the Tempest died within Keren Var, alongside the soldiers she had led into battle. Meredis Long-Standing died there with the defenders, as did Arven the Fourth, the guild leaders, the other hostages and all those trapped in the fortress. There were no survivors from within its walls. The Aurelate had recovered Morowa, but its Beacon and the people she had come to retrieve were dead. The uprising had ended in a catastrophe that neither side could call a victory.

The destruction reached the bridge as well. When the rock supporting its far anchorage collapsed, the half nearest Keren Var tore away and plunged into the ravine below. Only the half attached to the opposing cliff remained, extending out over the ravine and pointing toward the fallen citadel. Hedera's bridge had stood complete for less than a night. It had carried one army across and would never carry anyone back. The earth eventually ceased trembling, the dust began to drift away, and the sounds of battle stopped. No soldiers emerged, no defenders called from the ruins, and no more magic lit the dark. Only silence remained.

## Nyra's Tomb

Nyra's death ended the reign of the ninth Beacon. Her successor was her son Loras, the tenth Beacon of the Aurelate, who was still a young man when he inherited the title. He wanted to recover his mother's remains and give her a proper burial. To do so would require rebuilding a crossing and sending an expedition into the collapsed citadel. Hedera Durrn advised against the attempt, as did the mages who served Loras. No one could say what magic had destroyed Keren Var, whether any of its force remained, or whether the ruins had become dangerous or cursed. The only way to know would be to venture into the place from which no one had survived.

Loras was eventually persuaded not to risk more lives. No second bridge was commissioned, and his mother's body remained beneath the fallen fortress with the bodies of the rebels, soldiers, rulers and hostages. Later Beacons left the decision undisturbed. The citadel remained inaccessible, and Hedera's broken crossing was never restored.
