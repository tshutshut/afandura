The Stonewall Campaign

Long before the founding of the Aurelate, Orcs had already established themselves in the northwestern reaches of Ixoria. They had come from lands far beyond the continent and, over many generations, built fortified settlements throughout the region. Their strongholds became permanent homes rather than temporary outposts, and the Orcs gradually developed a territory of their own.

They were never entirely isolated from the other peoples of Ixoria. Trade, disputes, alliances, raids, and intermarriage connected the Orcish settlements to neighbouring communities. Children born of Orcish and other ancestry became part of the population of the region, and in time Half-Orcs could be found among both Orcish and non-Orcish communities. For generations, however, the Orcs remained independent of any larger power.

The rise of the Aurelate changed this balance.

As the Beacon Dynasty expanded across Ixoria, Aurelate territory gradually approached the lands held by the Orcs. Under Caelum the Unifier, the empire sought to bring additional territories into its rule largely through diplomacy. The Orcs were approached and offered the opportunity to enter the Aurelate under the authority of the Beacon. They refused.

Whether the Aurelate ever intended to destroy the Orcish settlements remains uncertain. The Orcs did not wait to discover the answer. They regarded the continuing expansion of the empire as an increasing threat to their independence and chose to expand their own territory rather than allow the Aurelate to reach their borders unchallenged.

The resulting expansion brought the two powers into direct conflict.

Under Caelum, attempts were still made to resolve the dispute without a prolonged war. The Orcs remained unwilling to submit, however, and fighting eventually spread across the contested territories. When Tiber the Watchful succeeded Caelum, the conflict had become a matter of imperial security. Tiber committed the resources of the Aurelate to a sustained military campaign against the Orcish territories.

The Orcs proved difficult to defeat. Their strongholds had been established generations earlier, their warriors knew the surrounding country, and their defensive positions were built to withstand prolonged attacks. The Aurelate possessed greater numbers and greater resources, however, and the Veilsteps allowed Tiber to move reinforcements across distances that the Orcs could not match. The war became increasingly costly for both sides.

The decisive confrontation came at Stonewall Keep.

Stonewall Keep guarded an enormous dam and reservoir known to the Orcs as Ghar-Urok. The structure had been built and maintained over generations, controlling the waters that sustained settlements and farmland throughout the surrounding region. Its importance was well understood by the Orcs, who had built the keep beside it to protect the dam and the territory dependent upon it.

The Aurelate army laid siege to Stonewall Keep and repeatedly assaulted its defenses. The Orcs held their position despite suffering heavy losses. The Aurelate suffered greatly as well, but its armies could be reinforced through the Veilsteps, while the Orcs had no comparable reserve of warriors.

The battle continued until both sides understood that the outcome could no longer be decided by a single assault. The Orcs could hold the Keep, but they could not replace their dead indefinitely. The Aurelate could continue the campaign, but taking the fortress would come at an increasingly severe cost.

Then the Orcs revealed the final measure available to them.

They threatened to destroy Ghar-Urok.

If the dam were deliberately breached, the reservoir would be released upon the lands below. The resulting flood would not distinguish between Orcish and Aurelate settlements. Villages, farmland, roads, mills, and other communities throughout the region would be destroyed. The Orcs themselves would lose much of their homeland in the process.

The threat was therefore not a promise of victory. It was a refusal to allow the Aurelate to claim a victory that would leave the region devastated.

Tiber ultimately chose negotiation.

The Stonewall Accord brought the conflict to an end. The Orcs agreed to abandon their territories and leave Ixoria rather than submit to Aurelate rule. In return, the Aurelate agreed to end its campaign and cease pursuing the departing Orcs. Ghar-Urok would remain standing, and the lands surrounding Stonewall Keep would not be destroyed in the final act of the war.

The Half-Orcs of Ixoria were given a separate choice. Those of Orcish ancestry were permitted either to leave the continent with the departing Orcs or to remain. Some chose to leave. Others remained, including individuals whose loyalties during the war had been divided between their Orcish ancestry and the lands in which they had been born. Those who stayed became part of the peoples of post-war Ixoria.

The Orcs therefore disappeared from Ixoria as a territorial and political power, but Orcish blood did not disappear with them.

The Stonewall Accord ended the war, but it never settled how the conflict should be remembered. Among Half-Orcs in particular, the Battle of Stonewall Keep remains a divisive subject. To some, it marks the defeat and exile of their ancestors. To others, it represents the moment in which the Orcs chose survival over a war they could no longer win.

Long after the last Orcish armies had departed, the name of Stonewall remained.

And for those who remember the history, it is enough to say:

Remember the Battle of Stonewall Keep.
