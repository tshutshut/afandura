# CM - Casting Spells

# Purpose

This chapter defines the spellcasting system for the Minimal Asynchronous Discord RPG.

Unlike traditional tabletop RPGs, magic is not limited by spell slots or mana pools. Instead, a spell becomes more reliable and easier to cast as the character gains experience with that specific spell.

---

# 1. Core Principles

The spell system is built around two simple questions:

1. **How difficult is the spell?**
2. **How well does the character know that spell?**

Every spell has an inherent **Complexity** that never changes.

Every character has an individual **Proficiency** for each spell they know. Two characters may know the same spell while having completely different proficiency levels.

Players are encouraged to think of magic as a craft that is mastered over time rather than a resource that is consumed.

There are:
- No spell slots.
- No distinction between cantrips and spells.
- No universal daily casting limit.

Instead, each spell defines its own usage limitations, if any.

---

# 2. Spell Stat Block

Every spell follows the same template.

- Name
- Complexity (Tier I–V)
- Proficiency (tracked individually by each character)
- Range
- Area of Effect
- Duration
- Damage or Effect
- Usage Limits (Unlimited, Once per Long Rest, etc.)
- Description

Only the **Proficiency** changes during play.

---

# 3. Spell Complexity

Complexity represents how inherently difficult a spell is to cast.

**Tier I**
- Simple spells.
- Easy to learn.
- Quickly mastered.

**Tier II**
- Basic adventuring spells requiring some practice.

**Tier III**
- Advanced spells requiring significant training.

**Tier IV**
- Powerful spells requiring considerable magical expertise.

**Tier V**
- Legendary or exceptionally difficult spells mastered only after long experience.

Complexity never changes.

---

# 4. Spell Proficiency

Every spell known by a character has its own proficiency level.

- Novice
- Apprentice
- Practiced
- Expert
- Master

Proficiency represents the caster's familiarity with that particular spell.

A newly learned spell always begins at **Novice**, regardless of the character's overall experience.

As the spell is used successfully in meaningful situations, proficiency increases.

---

# 5. Casting Difficulty

Compare the spell's Complexity with the caster's Proficiency.

| Spell Tier | Novice | Apprentice | Practiced | Expert | Master |
|------------|---------|------------|-----------|--------|--------|
| Tier I | Easy | Easy | Automatic | Automatic | Automatic |
| Tier II | Moderate | Easy | Easy | Automatic | Automatic |
| Tier III | Hard | Moderate | Easy | Easy | Automatic |
| Tier IV | Very Hard | Hard | Moderate | Easy | Easy |
| Tier V | Nearly Impossible | Very Hard | Hard | Moderate | Easy |

Difficulty thresholds:

- Automatic — No roll required.
- Easy — d20 ≥ 6
- Moderate — d20 ≥ 10
- Hard — d20 ≥ 14
- Very Hard — d20 ≥ 17
- Nearly Impossible — Natural 20 only.

A critical success or critical failure follows the normal game rules and is interpreted by the Dungeon Master.

---

# 6. Spell Progression

A spell becomes more familiar through **meaningful successful casts**.

Examples include:
- Combat.
- Solving puzzles.
- Overcoming obstacles.
- Helping allies.
- Advancing the story.

Repeated casting without narrative purpose does **not** contribute to progression.

The Dungeon Master has the final say on whether a successful cast counts.

Track progress for each spell individually.

Example:

Fireball (Tier III)

- Proficiency: Apprentice
- Progress: 7 / 18 Successful Casts

When the required number of successful casts is reached, the spell advances to the next proficiency level.

| Spell Tier | Novice → Apprentice | Apprentice → Practiced | Practiced → Expert | Expert → Master |
|------------|--------------------:|-----------------------:|-------------------:|----------------:|
| Tier I | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 |
| Tier II | 8 | 14 | 20 | 28 |
| Tier III | 10 | 18 | 26 | 36 |
| Tier IV | 14 | 24 | 34 | 46 |
| Tier V | 18 | 30 | 42 | 56 |
