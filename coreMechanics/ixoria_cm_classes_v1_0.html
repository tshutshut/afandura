Ixoria — Classes

Classes in Ixoria are story-driven archetypes rather than rigid professions. Choosing a class should say something meaningful about how a character has lived, what they have learned, and how they tend to approach the world. The questions beneath each class are intended to help players establish that history.

Barbarian

A Barbarian is someone whose approach to danger and adversity relies upon physical power, endurance, instinct, and an ability to continue when others would falter. The name does not imply stupidity, ignorance, or an inability to reason. A Barbarian may be intelligent, perceptive, and even highly knowledgeable, but their life and experiences have taught them to confront problems directly rather than delicately.

Barbarians tend to lack social refinement. They are often blunt, direct, impatient with elaborate etiquette, and sometimes vulgar. They may understand what others expect of them perfectly well while seeing little reason to disguise their thoughts or soften their words. This can make them uncomfortable participants in situations requiring diplomacy, subtle persuasion, or careful social maneuvering.

Physical confrontation, however, is familiar territory. Barbarians tend to relish opportunities to test their strength, endurance, and courage, and may feel more comfortable facing an immediate physical threat than navigating a room full of carefully chosen words.

Questions to Consider

  • What conditions or experiences shaped your character into a Barbarian?
  • Where did their physical resilience come from?
  • What kinds of conflicts have they experienced?
  • Is there a particular battle, fight, or confrontation they remember vividly?
  • How does your character fight? Have they developed a recognizable style or method?
  • What do they enjoy about physical confrontation?
  • How does their bluntness affect their relationships?
  • How do they react when a problem cannot be solved through strength or direct confrontation?

Bard

A Bard is, above all, an entertainer and performer. They have learned to use artistic expression not merely to display skill, but to awaken emotion in others. Through performance, a Bard can change the atmosphere of a room, capture an audience's attention, strengthen an existing feeling, or draw buried emotions to the surface.

The form this performance takes is entirely up to the character. A Bard might sing, chant, play an instrument, dance, tell stories, recite poetry, act, paint, or practice another form of artistic expression. What matters is not the medium, but the character's ability to communicate through it and affect those who experience their work.

A Bard's influence need not be cheerful. A performance might inspire courage, hope, enthusiasm, or joy, but might just as easily evoke sorrow, melancholy, nostalgia, anger, unease, or fear. Their art should form a meaningful part of who they are.

Questions to Consider

  • What is your Bard's primary form of artistic expression?
  • How did they learn their craft, and why is it important to them?
  • Does their performance require an instrument, costumes, paints, props, or other equipment?
  • Do they have a particular style, appearance, or persona?
  • What does their repertoire consist of, and where does the material come from?
  • What emotions do they most enjoy provoking?
  • What kind of performer are they?
  • How comfortable are they when they are not the center of attention?
  • How do they use social confidence when confronted with hostility?
  • When a situation becomes physical, how do they respond?
  • Is there a performance they are especially proud of, or one that went terribly wrong?
  • What audience do they most enjoy performing for?

Devout

A Devout is someone whose life is deeply shaped by religious devotion. They have a principal source of faith—a deity, sacred figure, or established religious tradition—and have dedicated a significant part of their life to understanding, practicing, and serving that faith. Simply believing or occasionally participating in religious customs does not make someone Devout.

A Devout follows the traditions, laws, rituals, duties, and restrictions associated with their faith. These obligations may influence what they eat, how they dress, how they treat others, the rituals they perform, the places they visit, or the actions they consider acceptable.

The Devout also seek to reinforce their faith through experience. They may interpret events through its teachings, search for meaning in hardship or coincidence, and invoke their deity during moments of fear, uncertainty, gratitude, inspiration, or need. Their devotion may be compassionate, contemplative, austere, disciplined, zealous, or uncompromising.

Faith and magic are entirely separate in Ixoria. Religious devotion does not grant magical ability, and possessing magic does not imply any religious connection.

Questions to Consider

  • What deity, sacred figure, or religious tradition does your character follow?
  • Why did they devote themselves to this faith?
  • What does their faith require of them in everyday life?
  • What rituals or observances do they practice?
  • What duties, prohibitions, or restrictions do they follow?
  • Have they sacrificed anything important to remain faithful?
  • How do they treat people who do not share their beliefs?
  • When do they pray or invoke their faith most often?
  • What experiences have strengthened their faith?
  • Has their devotion brought them into conflict with others?
  • How would you characterize the nature of their devotion?
  • What could cause them to question their faith?

Druid

A Druid is someone whose bond with the living world is central to who they are. That connection is not merely academic or a matter of belief. It is something they seek out and maintain through direct experience: observing animals, tending plants, experiencing weather, travelling through open spaces, and learning the patterns of the living world firsthand.

A Druid's connection may center upon a particular aspect of nature. One may feel most at home among forests and trees, another beside the sea, beneath an open sky, among mountains, across plains, or in the company of particular animals.

Druids often develop personal rituals, routines, or practices that nurture this connection. These need not be religious. A Druid is not defined by abstract knowledge about nature, but by wanting to experience and participate in it.

Questions to Consider

  • What part of the natural world does your Druid feel most connected to, and why?
  • Is their connection centered on a particular landscape, weather, animals, plants, or something else?
  • Are there particular animals or forms of life toward which they feel especially drawn?
  • Tell of a moment when they felt particularly connected to the natural world.
  • Where do they feel most at home?
  • What do they do regularly to nurture this connection?
  • Do they have personal rituals, routines, or seasonal practices?
  • How did those practices develop?
  • Did someone teach them, or did they learn through experience?
  • Is there a place in nature that is particularly important to them?
  • How do they react when forced to remain away from nature?
  • Is there any aspect of nature that frightens or challenges them?

Fighter

A Fighter is someone who has deliberately chosen to pursue martial skill as a discipline. Unlike a Barbarian, whose prowess may emerge from hardship or instinct, a Fighter has consciously sought training. Their abilities are the product of study, instruction, repetition, and practical experience under masters, schools, military traditions, or other martial doctrines.

This training should have given the Fighter a recognizable specialty: a particular weapon, combination of weapons, fighting style, or approach to combat. Their training also extends beyond physical technique. Their tradition should have imparted a doctrine—principles concerning how their skills are practiced, understood, and applied. These principles need not be moral or honorable.

Choosing Fighter means giving the character a genuine martial history. Their training should have teachers, methods, experiences, and consequences.

Questions to Consider

  • Why did your character choose martial training?
  • Who trained them?
  • Did they remain within one tradition or move between teachers and schools?
  • What fighting style did they learn?
  • What weapon, weapons, or form of combat do they specialize in?
  • What does their martial doctrine teach beyond technique?
  • What principles from that doctrine do they uphold?
  • Did their training impose duties, restrictions, loyalties, debts, or expectations?
  • Have they practiced their skills professionally?
  • Who have they served, fought for, or been hired by?
  • What experience best demonstrated the value of their training?
  • Is there a confrontation of which they are particularly proud?
  • Have they used their martial skill in a way they later regretted?
  • How has actual violence changed their understanding of their doctrine?

Vindicator

A Vindicator is a warrior whose martial life is inseparable from their faith. They have devoted considerable time to learning how to fight, but unlike a Fighter, martial mastery is not an end in itself. The Vindicator fights because they believe their faith demands action.

A Vindicator possesses a strong understanding of right and wrong shaped by religious conviction. They believe certain people, principles, places, traditions, or ways of life deserve protection, while other actions or forces must be confronted. They believe there are circumstances in which their ideals must be upheld by force.

Vindicators often take to the road, pursuing causes, answering perceived injustices, defending what they consider sacred, confronting enemies of their beliefs, and seeking opportunities to further their religious mission. Their certainty does not mean they are objectively correct. They may be admired as protectors by some and regarded as fanatical, dangerous, or misguided by others.

Questions to Consider

  • What faith does your Vindicator follow?
  • What teachings shape their understanding of right and wrong?
  • What do they believe must be protected?
  • What do they believe must be opposed?
  • Why do they believe physical force is sometimes necessary?
  • Who taught them to fight?
  • What weapons, styles, or techniques have they mastered?
  • Is their fighting tradition connected to their faith?
  • Does their faith impose rules on their use of violence?
  • What mission currently drives them through Ixoria?
  • Describe an important past mission in which they fought for their beliefs.
  • What consequences followed?
  • What conflict do they consider their greatest triumph?
  • Have they undertaken a mission that ended badly?
  • Have they ever questioned whether a righteous action truly served their faith?
  • How do they respond to believers who reject their militant interpretation?
  • What could cause them to abandon a mission they sincerely believe righteous?

Adventurer

An Adventurer is someone who has chosen a life of travel, exploration, and personal freedom. They are drawn toward unfamiliar places and new experiences, preferring the uncertainty of the road to the predictability of a settled existence. The journey itself is an important part of who they are.

Through experience, Adventurers develop practical knowledge of terrain, weather, animals, food, shelter, danger, and adaptation. Their knowledge of nature may resemble that of a Druid, but the motivation is different. A Druid seeks connection with the natural world; an Adventurer learns about it by travelling through it and surviving within it.

Adventurers develop particular affinities based upon where their travels have taken them. Their experience should reflect where they have actually been rather than granting universal knowledge of every environment. They should also have stories: expeditions, discoveries, accidents, encounters, and unexpected events that have shaped them.

Above all, Adventurers value freedom.

Questions to Consider

  • Why did your character choose a wandering life?
  • Where in Ixoria have they travelled extensively?
  • Which regions or routes do they know well?
  • What terrain or climate are they most comfortable in?
  • Is there an environment they dislike or know poorly?
  • What practical survival skills have they developed?
  • Do they travel alone or with others?
  • Have they worked in a profession connected to travel?
  • Describe a memorable expedition or journey.
  • What unexpected event occurred?
  • How did it change them?
  • What travel story do they particularly enjoy telling?
  • Is there a place they desperately want to revisit?
  • Is there somewhere they have always wanted to explore?
  • What equipment do they consider indispensable?
  • What does personal freedom mean to them?
  • Why have they never settled permanently?
  • What could make them abandon the wandering life?

Rogue

A Rogue is someone who chooses to live according to their own rules. Laws, traditions, authorities, and social expectations do not automatically command their obedience. Instead, a Rogue decides which rules deserve respect, which can be ignored, and which should be broken.

This does not necessarily make a Rogue a criminal. One might be a thief or outlaw, another a vigilante, and another simply a free spirit. What matters is personal independence and a set of self-defined boundaries.

Adaptability and resourcefulness are central to the Rogue's identity. Rogues improvise, observe their surroundings, exploit opportunities, circumvent obstacles, and find unconventional solutions. A Rogue must also possess practical skills that support this lifestyle. They might be an escape artist, understand locks and mechanisms, disable traps, move unnoticed, disguise themselves, deceive others, forge documents, perform sleight of hand, or possess some other specialty.

These abilities require explanation. The player should establish how the Rogue acquired and practiced them and when those abilities proved important.

Questions to Consider

  • What kind of lifestyle does your Rogue lead?
  • What rules or principles have they established for themselves?
  • Which laws, traditions, or expectations do they refuse to respect?
  • Where do they draw the line?
  • Were they always this independent?
  • What practical skills do they specialize in?
  • How did they acquire those skills?
  • Who, if anyone, taught them?
  • How long have they practiced?
  • When did one of those skills prove particularly useful?
  • What experience best demonstrates their resourcefulness?
  • Describe an unconventional solution they used to escape or overcome a problem.
  • Have their choices placed them in conflict with authority?
  • Have they deliberately broken an important rule? What happened?
  • Do they operate alone or with others?
  • Have they belonged to a gang, band, network, organization, vigilante group, or other community?
  • Who were their important companions?
  • What defining moment shaped them?
  • Has their independence cost them something important?
  • What could persuade them to follow somebody else's rules?

Mage

A Mage is someone born with an affinity for magic who has chosen to understand, study, and deliberately develop that ability. Magical potential exists from birth, but it does not necessarily express itself immediately. What defines the Mage is what they choose to do once they become aware of it.

For a Mage, magic is something to be understood. They experiment, observe effects, test limitations, and learn to reproduce desired results. Even a self-taught Mage progresses through conscious effort rather than simply surrendering to instinct.

Every Mage possesses an innate magical affinity that provides the foundation from which their abilities develop. Their education may be self-directed, guided by a mentor, or formalized through a college, institution, household, or other organization devoted to magical study.

Magic also manifests physically upon those who possess it. A Mage bears some physical expression of their magical nature. Magic may also have a history within their family: they may be the first known magically gifted individual in their lineage or descend from others who possessed magical abilities.

Ultimately, a Mage is defined by deliberate magical development. Their magic is innate, but their mastery of it is earned.

Questions to Consider

  • When did your character first consciously experience magic?
  • What happened during that first manifestation?
  • Did anyone else witness it?
  • How did they initially react?
  • What is their magical affinity?
  • How did they discover it?
  • How does their magic physically manifest?
  • Is that manifestation always visible?
  • How did they begin learning control?
  • What experiments or exercises did they perform?
  • What important failures occurred?
  • What discoveries are they proud of?
  • What can they now accomplish that they once could not?
  • Are they self-taught or did someone teach them?
  • If they had a mentor, who were they?
  • Have they studied at a magical institution?
  • What do they still want to understand?
  • Are they the first magically gifted individual in their family?
  • If not, who else possessed magic?
  • How far back does that lineage extend?
  • Did relatives possess similar affinities or manifestations?
  • How did their family react?
  • Has their pursuit of understanding caused an accident or harm?
  • Is there something about their magic they fear or avoid studying?

Warlock

A Warlock is someone born with a magical affinity who experiences and develops that magic primarily through instinct, emotion, and raw feeling. Like a Mage, their potential exists from birth and may reveal itself early or remain dormant until much later. The difference lies in how they relate to it.

Where a Mage seeks understanding, a Warlock experiences resonance. Their magic responds to what they feel. Fear, anger, grief, joy, desperation, love, exhilaration, or other intense emotional states may bring their abilities closer to the surface. They learn less by analyzing magic than by recognizing how it moves through them and discovering how to reach the emotional and instinctive states that allow them to express it.

This does not mean every Warlock is unstable or irrational. A skilled Warlock may possess considerable self-control, but their mastery is experiential rather than intellectual. For many, a defining magical experience may be connected to trauma, fear, loss, violence, or another profoundly negative event, though trauma is not required. Wonder, joy, love, exhilaration, or another moment of extraordinary intensity may also awaken their power.

Every Warlock possesses an innate magical affinity, and their magic manifests physically upon them. Magic may also form part of their lineage.

A Warlock is not simply a Mage who refused to study. A Mage attempts to understand magic from the outside inward; a Warlock learns it from the inside outward. Their strength lies in their willingness to feel and act instinctively, while their danger may come when the emotions through which they reach their magic begin to overwhelm them.

Questions to Consider

  • When did your character first consciously experience magic?
  • What happened?
  • What were they feeling?
  • Did anyone else witness it?
  • Was it connected to trauma or another life-changing event?
  • How did that event shape them?
  • If not trauma, what intense experience first brought magic to the surface?
  • What is their magical affinity?
  • How does that affinity physically manifest?
  • Does the manifestation change with emotion or magical intensity?
  • Which emotions bring their magic closest to the surface?
  • Which emotions make it stronger, weaker, or harder to control?
  • Can they deliberately summon the emotional state needed to use magic?
  • What does using magic feel like?
  • Do they enjoy releasing it, fear it, or both?
  • Have they ever completely lost control?
  • What happened, and who was affected?
  • Is there an emotion they fear experiencing because of what their magic might do?
  • How has magic changed their relationship with their own emotions?
  • Did anyone teach them to manage their abilities?
  • Are they the first magically gifted individual in their family?
  • If not, who else possessed magic?
  • Did relatives experience magic similarly?
  • How did their family react?
  • Do they want greater control, or fear that too much control would diminish their power?
